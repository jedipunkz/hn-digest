## Original Extract

Bakers

In a small town, there was a bakers' club popular with professionals and hobbyists alike. Every Saturday,
they would gather at the local cafe and bring their homemade cakes and pies; they ate, drank coffee, talked,
and exchanged the latest recipes. It was nice.
One day, a new visitor arrived from out of town. He didn't say a word, didn't bring any cake, but tasted
every cake there was and picked out the best pieces to take home with him. The regulars were confused, but
shrugged it off and moved on with their lives.
The following week, he came back, drank all the coffee, took every single cake, and left again. People
tried to talk to him, but he would not listen. This was unacceptable, so the group decided not to open the
door for him next time.
A week later, he visited again, but the door was locked. He banged on the door, demanded access to the
cakes, even cracked the glass a little, and screamed that cake was free, so he had a right to take it. But
the shocked people in the cafe did not open the door, so he left. The bakers talked and speculated about what
a single person would want so many cakes for. They had no idea.
The next week, almost two dozen new visitors arrived. All from different towns, all at the same time. The
parking lot was so full that many regulars could not find a spot. But the new visitors looked normal, behaved
normally, even brought some store-bought donuts, so they were let in. The event was open for visitors after
all. But something was off. They didn't speak much, didn't share any of their own recipes, and no one knew
them. At the end of the evening, each of them took just enough cake not to look suspicious. In the end, all
the cakes were gone, and the regulars looked at their empty plates in confusion. Cake is meant to be eaten,
but this was wrong.
The Bakers' Club held an extraordinary meeting. They had to do something, so they decided to only accept
visitors from now on if they bring their own homemade cake. Also, everyone was allowed to take home only one
slice. The news spread quickly. People who regularly enjoyed the pleasant atmosphere but couldn't bake
themselves felt they were being treated unfairly. After all, they hadn't done anything wrong! They just
wanted to enjoy some cake.
The Saturday after the meeting, it was even more crowded than the week before. There was not a single
parking spot left in the entire street. The new people were locals and even brought cakes this time, but they
looked strangely familiar and odd at the same time. Some cakes were a mixture of many different recipes and
tasted too sweet or too dry. Others were burned, some had collapsed, one cake was made with salt instead of
sugar, and another was beautifully decorated, but its core was made out of cardboard. The regular members
looked at the cakes and tasted them, but they were all awful. The new people who brought them still looked
proud, praised the new cooking app they all seemed to use, and didn't accept any advice. "The new app knows
more than you," they said. "We don't need to be bakers to bake a cake! The app tells us what to do," they
said. They couldn't even understand what was wrong with their cakes.
Most of the regulars didn't come back the next week. Too much noise, too many people who couldn't really
bake and didn't want to learn. The parking situation was horrible, and every bite was a gamble. Most cakes
were awful; some were dangerous. The recipes were unoriginal, flawed, useless. Nothing special, nothing new.
People once came to the bakers' club to get new ideas and inspiration, talk to peers, and learn from each
other to become better bakers. But that was no longer possible. Too many people relied on the new app. They
couldn't kick them all out. It was a lost cause.
Months later, the cafe is still busy every Saturday. The app has improved a lot, especially after that one
week when several people were hospitalized with food poisoning after eating cake. The cakes are mostly edible
now, and some are genuinely beautiful, but they still do not taste very good. People proudly show off their
creations, talk about decoration and style or the new app version, but rarely taste them or take slices home
for later. Most cakes go to the trash after the event. No one talks about recipes anymore. But they seem to
have fun.
Some of the old bakers have started a new club and rented a small private kitchen. Members only, but you
can bring friends. No one is required to show up with a finished cake anymore. Instead, they bring
ingredients, ideas, and questions. They bake together, only one or two cakes an evening. They learn, share
experiences, talk about the time when the app took over their old club, and enjoy some cake. It may not be
the best or most beautiful cake ever. Sometimes they have to order pizza, but even then they learn something.
And next Saturday, they try again.
