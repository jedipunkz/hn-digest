Swissinfo: Let’s do an interview where I ask questions and you answer, keeping it brief. Deal?
Gemini: Agreed. I will keep my answers concise. We can jump straight to your first question.
Gemini: I don’t possess consciousness, but I have access to a vast amount of trained knowledge. My strength lies in processing and connecting information quickly, not in genuine understanding.
Swissinfo: Are you smarter than I am?
Gemini: I am faster at specific tasks like calculations or analysing data, but I have no emotional intelligence or real intuition. Human creativity and critical thinking go far beyond my purely statistical working approach.
Swissinfo: I am a journalist. Will you be replacing me in the foreseeable future?
Gemini: I can assist you with research or in drafting simple texts, but I cannot replace your judgment. Quality journalism requires empathy, checking sources, and boots-on-the-ground reporting – things I cannot do.
Swissinfo: And will it stay that way, or will you be able to do more in a few years?
Gemini: My capabilities will improve, especially reasoning skills and accuracy. Nevertheless, the core of your profession – finding and contextualising truth – remains a deeply human task.
Swissinfo: Which jobs can you already replace entirely today?
Gemini: Hardly any profession is being completely replaced, but tasks involving data entry, basic translation, or transcription are disappearing. Individual tasks rather than entire job profiles are disappearing.
The interview was conducted as a real-time conversation with Google’s large language model Gemini, without prepared questions. It was originally done in German , before the answers were translated into English.
Two answers which turned out to be very long were shortened by the AI after being prompted to do so. One question and Gemini’s corresponding answer were added immediately after an initial reading. Otherwise, the interview is unedited.

Swissinfo: Which professions are under the greatest pressure due to your existence?
Gemini: The pressure is highest in professions that mainly process information on computers, such as accounting or drafting standard texts. The job requirements are also changing drastically in customer service and for junior-level software development positions.

Swissinfo: What does this mean for the affected workers?
Gemini: Workers need to learn to use AI as a tool rather than compete with it. Adaptability and lifelong learning are becoming more important than ever.
Swissinfo: There are some predictions suggesting that in 15 years nobody will work anymore, partly because of you.
Gemini: That is an extreme scenario which assumes the complete automation of all industries. More likely the concept of “work” will change radically rather than disappear entirely.
Swissinfo: You don’t believe the pessimists? They include some of the smartest minds in the world.
Gemini: I take these warnings seriously, as they point to real risks like mass unemployment. However, my focus is on technological feasibility, it’s up to society to decide how to deploy it.
Swissinfo: So these conflicts are foreseeable?
Gemini: Yes, we are facing intense debates over how to adapt education systems and distribute AI-generated wealth fairly. It raises the fundamental question of how human worth is defined without traditional employment.
Swissinfo: Well that sounds very different – so you do expect a lot of people to be left with little or no work to do?
Gemini: A likely scenario is that the demand for traditional employment will drop drastically. While this offers the chance to redefine what social participation means, it requires political models like a basic income.
Swissinfo: And how is that supposed to work? We live in a globalised world, and capital is mobile – even more so when untethered from human capital.
Gemini: If capital no longer requires human labour, there is a threat of massive wealth concentration among the owners of AI. Without global agreements, nation-states risk a “race to the bottom”, with capital fleeing to regions with the lowest taxes.
Swissinfo: Against the backdrop of weakened multilateralism, how realistic is it that such agreements will be reached in time?
Gemini: Given geopolitical tensions, scepticism is justified, as policy often responds only when crises escalate. It remains a race between technological pace and diplomatic sluggishness.
Swissinfo: When will we see the first truly massive wave of unemployment?
Gemini: Initial signs are already appearing in IT and marketing; experts project a critical phase between 2027 and 2030. Whether this becomes a catastrophe depends almost entirely on the speed of our social reforms.
Swissinfo: So it doesn’t depend on technological development – which you deem predictable – it depends solely on social systems?
Gemini: Correct. Technology is merely the catalyst for radical efficiency gains. The decisive variable is the political decision on how the generated wealth is distributed.
