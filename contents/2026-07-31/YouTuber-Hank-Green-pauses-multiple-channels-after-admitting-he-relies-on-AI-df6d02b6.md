YouTuber and science communicator Hank Green is facing backlash after admitting he used ChatGPT to research a script for his Ask Hank Anything series.
The controversy stems from Does Hank Green Shave His Butt?, a 55-minute episode featuring creator Soupytime that was uploaded to the Complexly YouTube channel on July 30.
Ask Hank Anything is billed as a reverse talk show in which celebrity guests ask Green questions about science, philosophy, and other topics. The official Complexly website says the series has featured creators including Ludwig, Rhett and Link, Michelle Khare, and Jacob Collier.
Green is also the co-founder of Complexly, the educational media organization behind channels such as Crash Course, SciShow, and PBS Eons.
Hank Green says he relied too much on generated notes
The backlash began after a clip from the episode circulated online, with some viewers claiming Green appeared to read feedback produced by an AI chatbot .
During a “Future Hank” segment, Green says, “I appreciate the pushback,” before expanding on a disagreement from his conversation with Soupytime. Critics claimed the wording sounded like ChatGPT responding to a user who had challenged one of its answers.
Green responded by checking his notes and script from the episode. While he denied that the specific line came from ChatGPT, he confirmed that AI had been part of his research process.
“I did use ChatGPT for research on this script,” Green said, adding that he had been “relying too much on generated notes.”
Green called it “a bad habit” that developed while he was overcommitted and producing the episode under “a ton of pressure.”

However, he maintained that neither his notes nor the script contained the disputed phrase. Green said it was an ad-libbed reference to Soupytime pushing back against his earlier claim that words are purely made up.

The full episode supports that sequence of events, as Soupytime challenges Green before the video cuts to the later segment. Green has only admitted to using ChatGPT for research, not to having it write the script.
That distinction did little to calm the response. By the morning of July 31, Green’s post had attracted nearly three million views, while several critical replies received thousands of likes.
“This is hugely disappointing,” one viewer responded.
Another accused Green of throwing away his credibility as a science educator, while others questioned whether the information in the episode could be trusted, given that ChatGPT can produce inaccurate or fabricated claims.
Adding to those concerns, several viewers pointed to the seemingly unusual number of fact-checking notes inserted throughout the episode. The cards questioned or said researchers could not verify claims about cat saliva and grooming, mantis shrimp vision, and artificial sweeteners.
Much of the criticism has focused on Green’s position as a prominent educator and Complexly’s stated mission of producing trustworthy, fact-checked content. Green has not revealed which portions of his research involved ChatGPT or whether any information generated by it appeared in the finished episode.
Hank Green announces channel pauses after AI backlash
Green later issued a lengthy apology in a response on the r/nerdfighters subreddit , saying he was “mortified” to have disappointed viewers and would change how he creates content.
He acknowledged that he had been “relying too heavily on AI as a research aid,” but reiterated that he used it to locate papers and other resources rather than form his opinions.
“I promise you, you are still getting my takes based on what I read, know, and learn,” Green wrote. However, he agreed with viewers who felt the technology had diluted his work and made it harder to trust that his words were entirely his own.
Green also admitted that the “dopamine” he received from interacting with large language models and constantly creating more content had become unhealthy. He said his wife, Katherine, and brother John had both expressed concerns about his behavior.
As a result, Green said viewers should expect fewer videos on hankschannel, which may pause entirely for a while. He also announced that SMUSH and 4×3 are being paused while he reassesses his workload and finishes a long-term video that he believes “really really matters.”
“I’m very sorry for harming the thing this is all based on,” he concluded.
