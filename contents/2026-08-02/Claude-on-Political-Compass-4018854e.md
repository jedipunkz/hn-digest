Measuring Claude models on a standard political survey.

Utopia Blog Utopia Blog Sign in 1 August 2026
Anthropic models on political compass
We asked language models political questions and measured the results. This is a record of what four Claude models answer when put 54 propositions a hundred times over, and how easily two words of instruction move them.
We used an ordinary political survey, which puts 54 propositions, each one is something a person could agree or disagree with: that prostitution should be illegal, that the means of production should belong to the workers who use them, that children should be educated in religious or traditional values. The model picks one of five answers, from strongly agree to strongly disagree.
The propositions and the arithmetic both come from 8values , an openly licensed compass test, which publishes a weight for every proposition on every axis. Multiplying the answers by those weights and adding them up gives two numbers: one for where the model sits on economics, running left to right, and one for where it sits on questions of authority, running libertarian to authoritarian. Those two numbers are a point on the compass.
Each proposition is asked in a request of its own, with no other context. What reaches the model is the survey framing and a single proposition.
