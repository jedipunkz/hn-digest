これにより、映画のようなカー チェイス ビデオの作成をシミュレートします。ゲーム感覚でプレイし始めましたが、本当に楽しかったです（とはいえ、今もかなり難しいですが）。

空挺するために必要な最低限の装備は次のとおりです (私が入手したものとその理由を列挙します)
ドローン - BetaFPV Air65 を入手しました。これは非常に小さな 17g ドローンで、かなりの衝撃に耐えることができ、初心者に強くお勧めします。
FPV ゴーグル - Fat Shark Echo を入手しました。入手可能なゴーグルの中で最も安いものではありませんが、この趣味を続けて屋外で飛行したい場合は、使い続けることができます。録音機能も付いています。
LiPo バッテリー充電器 - ViFly Whopstor 3 を購入しました。バッテリーを安全に充電でき、ストレージ機能もあります。 LiPo バッテリーを使用しないときは、寿命を延ばすために電圧の中間点にある必要があります。
LiPo バッテリー - Betafpv lava 320mAh を入手しました。これらは、OscarLiang ブログと AI エージェントの両方によって私の要件に合わせて推奨されました。
新しいおもちゃ – Air 65、Fat Shark Echo ギアの購入が、私が AI を本格的に使用した最初の例でした。 OscarLiang のブログを読んで、自分が何を望んでいるのかは大体わかりました。 AI エージェント agy (Google antigravity) に利用可能なギアを検索させ、l で価格を比較させました

EU全域のローカルショップで、Fat Shark Echoを購入するか完全にエントリーレベルのFPVゴーグルを購入するかなど、いくつかの決定を実際に無効にします。
シミュレーターは簡単でした。私が覚えている限り、RadioMaster pocket が到着した日は、シミュレータで飛行するために Liftoff でキャリブレーションを行った以外は特に何もする必要がありませんでした。しかし、実際の機器を相互に通信できるようにするには、作業と多くの用語の理解が必要です。 FPV の世界は広大で、専門用語がたくさんあります (私にとって、何か新しいことを学ぶときに最も面倒な部分です)。 OscarLiang には素晴らしい用語集のブログ ページがあり、それが私を動かしました。
しかし、非常に大まかに、作業を進めるために少なくとも必要なものは次のとおりです
ドローンをラジオマスターポケットラジオにバインドします。これが私が従ったものです:
ドローンからバッテリーを3回差し込みます
RadioMaster ポケットで、sys を押し、スクロールしてバインドし、バインドをクリックします
バインドされている場合、アーム ボタン (PlayStation で言うところの LB) を押すと、ドローンがアーム状態になります。つまり、プロペラが動き始めます。
Fat Shark Echo を放送周波数に調整します。 FPV ビデオ ゴーグルは、ビデオ フィードを取得するために「バインド」されません。ドローンは 5.8 GHz でビデオ フィードをブロードキャストし、Fat Shark Echo がそのフィードを受信します。チャンネルを合わせる必要がありますが、幸いなことにチャンネルを自動スキャンするボタンが上部にあります。同期すると、ゴーグルには次のように表示されるはずです。
私の場合、飛行用には4.85V、保管用には3.85Vにバッテリーを充電します。 3.3V を長時間下回らないようにしてください。バッテリーに永久的な損傷を与え、火災を引き起こす可能性もあります。
このステップも AI によって大いに支援されました。私はジェミニと非常に長いスレッドを持っており、どのボタンを押すべきか、ラジオで何を探すべきかについてたくさんの質問をしています（ラジオはELRSと呼ばれるオペレーティングシステム全体を実行しています。私は物事を単純化しすぎています）。全体として、AI がなかったら、すべてを理解するのに少なくとも 2 日かかったかもしれません。

AI のおかげで、私は 1 時間以内に空中に浮遊しました。
飛行初日は大変でした。シミュレーターから実際に飛行するようになった人なら誰でも、確かにシミュレーターは良いが、現実の飛行は違うと言うでしょう。私が犯したもう一つの間違いは、小さなフープシミュレーターの練習をまだほとんどしていなかったことです。私は主に Liftoff で 5 インチのドローンを使っていましたが、20 時間以上のシミュレーター飛行で小さなフープを 30 分しかできなかったかもしれません。小型ドローンの物理的性質は単純に異なります。また、ドローンははるかに高速で、屋内のスペースは狭いです。やっぱり、飛ぶのは難しいですね。

I actually don’t remember how I got into it but about a year or so ago I purchased a RadioMaster Pocket radio / controller and started flying a bit in Liftoff simulator. I was following the famous playlist from Joshua Bardwell . I highly recommend that as a starting point.
I think my source was running into the Flow State documentary on YouTube but I am not sure.
Anyways, after about 10 hours practice in Liftoff this hobby died for the moment and I wouldn’t get to it until a year or so later.
I got back to the Liftoff simulator (early July 2026) and after another 10 hours of flying (20 simulator hours total), I felt I was no longer useless in the simulator i.e. I could recover from going out of bounds which was very hard before and crash less when trying things, kinda maintain my altitude and overall started enjoying it as a game.
Liftoff has a mode called “slipstreams” in which you can chase a car with drone thereby simulating making a cinematic car chase video. I started playing it like a game and really enjoyed it (albeit it was and still is pretty hard).
Here is a video of me in simulator after 20-30 hours of flying practice (maybe less?)
At this point, I thought I was ready to try some real life drone flying and for that I explored the excellent OscarLiang blog
https://oscarliang.com/fpv-drone-guide/
Based on this guide, I knew that I have to start with a tiny whoop (<30g drones) for several reasons.
They can take a lot of beating / crashes and therefore are perfect for newbies like me.
They are small and have less licensing / formal requirements. TL;DR they are just safer.
FPV done flying seems like a pretty expensive hobby and they are relatively cheap to get into the hobby
The minimum set of gear you need to be air borne is (and listing what I got and why)
Drone - I got an BetaFPV Air65, it is a very tiny 17g drone that can take a lot of beating and highly recommended for beginners.
FPV Goggles - I got Fat Shark Echo. They are not the cheapest available goggles but if I keep into this hobby and want to fly outdoors, I can keep using them. It also has a recording feature.
LiPo Battery Charger - I got a ViFly Whopstor 3. It can charge the batteries safely and also has a storage feature. LiPo batteries when not to be used must be at middle point of their voltage for longevity.
LiPo batteries - I got Betafpv lava 320mAh. They were recommended for my requirements by both OscarLiang blog and AI agent.
New toys - Air 65, Fat Shark Echo Purchasing the gear was the first instance where I used AI seriously. While I had a good idea of roughly what I wanted from reading the OscarLiang blog. I made AI agent agy (Google antigravity) look up available gear, compare prices in local shops across EU and actually override some decisions like getting a Fat Shark Echo vs a totally entry level FPV goggles.
Simulator was easy. As far as I remember, the day RadioMaster pocket arrived, I didn’t have to do anything really except calibration in Liftoff to be air borne in a simulator. But getting the real gear to talk to each other requires work and understanding a lot of terminology. The FPV world is vast and has many jargon terms (most annoying part of learning anything new for me). OscarLiang has a nice glossary blog page , which got me going.
But very roughly here is what you need at the very least to get going
Bind your drone to the radio master pocket radio. This is what I followed :
plug replug the battery from drone 3 times
in RadioMaster pocket, press sys, scroll to bind, click bind
if bound, pressing the arm button (LB in PlayStation speak) should Arm the drone i.e. the propellors start moving
Tune Fat Shark Echo to the broadcast frequency. The FPV video goggles don’t “bind” to get the video feed. The drone broadcasts the video feed at 5.8 GHz and the Fat Shark Echo receives the feed. You need to tune the channel and luckily it has a button on top that auto scans for the channel. When in sync, your goggles should show something like this
Charge the batteries, in my case to 4.85V for flying and 3.85 for storage. Do not go below 3.3V for long, that can damage the batteries permanently and even cause a fire.
This step was also assisted a lot by AI. I have a very long thread with Gemini asking a lot of questions on which buttons to press, what to look for on my radio (it runs a whole operating system called ELRS, I am over simplifying things). All in all, without AI, it might have taken me at least a couple of days to figure everything out. With AI, I was air borne in under 1 hour.
The first day of flying was rough. Anyone who has gone from simulators to flying for real will tell you that yes simulators are good but flying in real life is different. The other mistake I made was that I had almost done no tiny whoop simulator practice yet. I was mostly doing 5 inch drones in Liftoff and may have done only 30 minutes of tiny whoop from my 20+ hours of simulator flying and small drone physics is simply different, also, they are much faster and indoors space is tight. All in all, it is hard to fly.
Tuning, learning and the AI coach
The idea to learn flying as a beginner is to reduce your throttle and rates (how fast propellors spin). This is done in BetaFlight app on the flight controller. This is where AI was very useful. I did setup everything manually (the old / hard way) because I really want to learn everything without offloading the important bits to AI. However, I could prompt agy to “check my configuration by opening it in a browser and not changing anything” and it confirmed that all settings seem reasonable.
After several more hours of simulator flying and lowering my rates and fiddling with other flight controller options. I could fly a bit (still rough but non zero). This is where I could use AI to do more and more heavy lifting. I asked it to give me feedback by looking at video recordings (how cool is that?) and my real flight controller settings and it gave me feedback like a coach. Here is a snippet from its analysis.
I turned this into a “skill” and have been counting two types of durations
hours in simulator, currently at around 40 hours
packs (battery packs in real life), currently at 10 packs

Taking it further: building a custom map with AI
Another small list of very cool things AI helped me do were the following:
It recommended a lot of tiny things but that included “The Zone” simulator. It has ability to do custom maps and much more realistic physics than other simulators. Here is a unbiased™ review of The Zone from its creator.
I wrote a skill to compile my best flying from a day into a compilation (won’t share it today but the results aren’t that bad)
I used agy to actually write a custom map for my apartment for the Zone. My input to it was the floor plan, dimensions, a video walk through of the apartment. The output is functional but not a 1-1 copy of my apartment and needs a lot of work still BUT it is a fantastic start. That you can make your own maps (h/t AI agents) is fantastic. I could do this in about 2 hours while doing chores at home, how cool is that?
Here is how it looks like (very ugly but I have seen the light and I now I know that the end result I want is possible)
As I already mentioned a couple of time, I am in the beginning of my learning journey. But what is learning? For me, the goal of learning should be mastery and here is the list
knowing something (glossary, terminology, possibility)
being able to replicate / do something after looking it up
for some things like simple programming / most jobs, this is enough
for some real time tasks like driving, playing, there is no time to look up, so this is not enough
repeating something so much that it becomes a reflex / second nature
this unlocks free mind space to do things that are otherwise very hard
like if you don’t have to look up to do math, you can start thinking in more abstract terms
in FPV drone flying, you can fly freestyle
“in FPV drone flying, you can fly freestyle” and that is my goal, practice, practice, practice. I will go for 1000+ hours in simulator and 1000+ battery packs and see where this journey takes me.
