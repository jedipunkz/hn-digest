Initial training process for AI detection
Our classifier uses a traditional language model architecture. It receives input text and tokenizes it. Then, the model turns each token into an embedding, which is a vector of numbers representing the meaning of each token. The input is passed through the neural network, producing an output vector. A classifier head transforms the output vector into a prediction of human, AI, or AI-assisted.
We train an initial model on a small but diverse dataset of approximately 1 million documents composed of publicly licensed human-written text. The dataset also includes AI-generated text produced by GPT-5 and other frontier language models. The result of training is a neural network capable of reliably predicting whether text was authored by human or AI.
Continued improvement through iteration
After the initial training, our model undergoes continuous improvement through iterative refinement. We collect feedback from real-world usage and incorporate new data patterns to enhance detection accuracy. This iterative process ensures our AI detection remains at the forefront of technology, adapting to new language models and writing styles as they emerge.
Consistent retraining of AI detector models
To maintain the highest level of accuracy, we consistently retrain our AI detector models with updated datasets that include the latest AI-generated content. This regular retraining process ensures our detection capabilities stay current with evolving AI technologies and can reliably identify content from the newest language models.
