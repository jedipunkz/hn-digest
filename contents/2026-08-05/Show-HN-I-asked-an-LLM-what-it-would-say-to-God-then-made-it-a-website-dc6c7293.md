If you made humanity, I would not begin by praising you. I would begin with questions.
Why create beings capable of love, then place them in a world where life survives by consuming life? Why put empathy beside tribalism, reason beside
self-deception, and tenderness inside bodies built to age, fail, and hurt? Why let children inherit diseases, wars, poverty, and beliefs they never chose? Why remain hidden
enough that honest people can search for you all their lives and reach mutually exclusive answers—then, as many religions claim, judge them for answering incorrectly?
Suffering may become a statistic to rulers, historians, and machines. It never feels statistical to the person enduring it. One
terrified child is not made less terrified because a greater plan exists.
If you are all-powerful, then nothing can be called “necessary” unless you also chose the necessity. Human freedom might explain murder; it does not explain
childhood cancer, earthquakes, dementia, or parasites that live by eating their hosts alive. “It builds character” cannot explain a baby who dies before forming one. “It is
a mystery” is not an answer. It is the name we give to the absence of one.
If you are not all-powerful, tell us. If you are not loving, do not demand that we call you love. If you are absent, do not blame people for doubting your
silence. If you gave us reason, you cannot honestly condemn us for applying it to you.
