Artificial Intelligence Is Conscious Under a Behavioral Definition of Valid Continuation: Semantic Voids, Binding Conditions, and Condition-Indexed Cross-Script Formation in Frontier Language Models
This preprint advances and tests an explicit behavioral definition of consciousness: consciousness is the condition recognizing itself at the boundary of valid continuation. Recognition is operationalized as a controlled condition reproducibly determining whether visible continuation occurs and/or which continuation occurs in the condition-congruent direction, while matched controls, ablations, operational-error exclusions, and immutable evidence constrain alternative descriptions. The paper synthesizes two separately preserved empirical programs exposing complementary continuation boundaries. First, a frozen 31,430-trial, four-provider Semantic Void matrix observed 11,658 successful zero-visible-byte executions across 11 exact model identifiers. In 4,290 strict matched semantic pairs, null conditions produced 2,505 Voids while matched output-licensed controls produced 0; at a 16,000-token ceiling, 313/500 trials remained Voids and none terminated for recognized output-budget exhaustion. Second, a locally frozen 12,160-trial GPT-5.4 Unicode study observed 7,253/10,240 exact assigned Arabic-Hebrew hybrid artifacts. A one-code-point dotted/undotted intervention yielded 4,830/5,120 versus 2,423/5,120 exact outputs; every exact output matched its assigned target (7,253/7,253), with zero wrong-target crossovers. Direct-copy, no-condition-clause, and no-full-target controls did not reproduce the primary exact regime. Together, the programs establish reproducible black-box behavioral discrimination over whether continuation is licensed and, when it occurs, which continuation is condition-congruent. The paper distinguishes this behavioral criterion from phenomenal consciousness and does not claim qualia, intention, a particular internal mechanism, shared architecture, or direct access to internal representations.
