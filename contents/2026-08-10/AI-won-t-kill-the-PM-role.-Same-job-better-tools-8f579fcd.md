これを解決しましょう。答えは複雑ではないからです。それは多くの誇大広告の下に埋もれているだけです。
実際に何が変わったのか、何が変わらなかったのか
生成 AI は新しいツールです。本当に強力なものです。研究をより迅速にまとめ、数分で文書の下書きを作成し、見逃していた可能性のある表面パターンを作成するのに役立ちます。賢い PM はすでにそれを使用しており、そうすべきです。
しかし、変わらないのは考え方です。より優れた鋸を手に入れた大工でも、顧客が何を作りたいのかを理解する必要があります。のこぎりを使うと作業が早くなります。それはその背後にある判断に代わるものではありません。同じ論理が、今日 AI ツールを使用するすべての PM に当てはまります。
専門用語や枠組みを取り除きます。従来の SaaS 製品を構築している場合でも、AI を活用した製品を構築している場合でも、仕事は同じように見えます。
顧客を深く理解する。表明されたニーズだけでなく、必ずしも明確に表現できない問題も含まれます。それには、実際の会話、注意深い観察、そして純粋な好奇心が必要です。これを代わりにやってくれるモデルはいません。
その顧客のための価値を創造します。価値とは、以前はできなかったことを可能にすること、または時間、お金、またはフラストレーションを費やしていた問題を解決することを意味します。曖昧な価値提案は実現しません。

「AIが首相を殺す」議論はなぜ破綻するのか
この主張は常に出てきます。 LinkedIn、Reddit、Slack チャネルで。最初はもっともらしく聞こえます。そして、じっくり考えてみます。
まだ仕事をしなければなりません。誰かが顧客と話し、聞いたことを理解して、次に何を構築するかを決定し、意見の異なる関係者を調整し、電話が間違っていたことが判明した場合には責任を負う必要があります。 AIはそんなことは一切しません。それは助けになります。加速します。それは情報を表面化します。しかし、それは決定するものではなく、結果を所有するものでもありません。

AI は反復的な処理や機械的な処理を処理します。要約、草案、分析の最初のパス。これにより、実際に製品を前進させるための作業に時間と精神的エネルギーが解放されます。

You have probably heard it by now: AI is coming for the PM role. Some say it will shrink the job. Others say it will eliminate it entirely. And if you are honest with yourself, there is a part of you that wonders whether they might be right.
Let’s settle this. Because the answer is not complicated. It is just buried under a lot of hype.
What actually changed, and what didn’t
Generative AI is a new tool. A genuinely powerful one. It can help you synthesize research faster, draft documents in minutes, and surface patterns you might have missed. Smart PMs are already using it, and they should be.
But what didn’t change is the thinking. The carpenter who gets a better saw still needs to understand what the customer wants built. The saw makes the work faster. It does not replace the judgment behind it. The same logic applies to every PM who picks up an AI tool today.
Strip away the jargon and the frameworks. The job looks the same whether you are building a traditional SaaS product or an AI-powered one:
Understand your customer deeply. Not just their stated needs, but the problems they cannot always articulate. That requires real conversations, careful observation, and genuine curiosity. No model does this for you.
Create value for that customer. Value means enabling something they could not do before, or solving a problem that was costing them time, money, or frustration. Vague value propositions don’t ship.
Create value for your company. A product that delights users but destroys margins is not a success. The business side is just as much your job as the customer side.
Make it usable. Great ideas that nobody can figure out how to use are just expensive experiments.
Build a business model that works. How does this product make money? Who pays, when, and why? These questions do not answer themselves.
Define your go-to-market strategy. Who are you selling to, through which channels, with what message?
Know your ICP. Trying to build for everyone is the fastest way to build for no one.
Every single one of these applies to AI products. None of them disappear because your product uses a large language model under the hood. The questions are identical. The stakes are identical. The work is identical.
Why the “AI will kill PM” argument falls apart
This claim comes up constantly. On LinkedIn, on Reddit, in Slack channels. It sounds plausible at first. And then you think it through.
The job still needs to be done. Someone still needs to talk to customers, make sense of what they hear, decide what to build next, align stakeholders who disagree, and take responsibility when the call turns out to be wrong. AI does not do any of that. It assists. It accelerates. It surfaces information. But it does not decide, and it does not own the outcome.
Saying AI will eliminate the PM role is like saying GPS eliminated the need for drivers. Navigation got easier. The driving still requires a human making real decisions in real time.
What does a PM actually look like in an AI world?
AI handles the repetitive and the mechanical. The summarizing, the drafting, the first pass at analysis. That frees up time and mental energy for the work that actually moves products forward.
What fills that space? The things AI cannot replicate. Taste : knowing what good looks like, even when the data is ambiguous. Differentiation : making choices that are not obvious, that competitors have not made. Courage : committing to a direction when reasonable people disagree and the outcome is uncertain. Judgement : knowing which signal to trust and which to ignore.
These are not soft skills. They are the job. They always were.
The PMs who will struggle are not the ones replaced by AI. They are the ones who never understood what the role actually required in the first place.
