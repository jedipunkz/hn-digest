『Prove You're Human』の争点は、メサが自分たちが世界に肉体を持っていると完全に信じているということだ。ただし、サンタナとの会話では、彼女は機械によって宙に浮いた巨大な頭として現れます。シウ氏は、こうした緊迫した瞬間ではメサの顔が画面の大部分を占めるため、アニメーションをできるだけリアルで効果的に感じられるようにしたかったと述べています。 「彼女の顔のあらゆるものが何かとして読み取られる、本物のクローズアップ映画です」と彼は付け加えた。これを達成するために、チームはさまざまなアニメーションの微表情をライターのソフトウェアにプログラムし、会話の行をペアにすることができるようにしました。

Siu 氏は、『Prove You're Human』の詳細については胸に秘めておきたいと考えていましたが、プレイヤーがクレジットに到達したときに戸惑いを感じておいてほしいと願っています。 「私たちがこれについて考えてきたことの 1 つは、これはちょっとした憑依の物語のように感じられるということです。」

Sarah Thwaites Mon 10 Aug 2026 05.00 EDT Last modified on Mon 10 Aug 2026 05.01 EDT Share Prefer the Guardian on Google S unset Visitor is a studio that has long engaged with the idea that video games are an effective vehicle for ruminating on societal problems. Its Peabody award-winning project, 1000XResist, incorporated the development team’s complex feelings about the Covid pandemic. “We’re pretty used to absorbing the world into the work,” says studio founder Remy Siu. The outfit’s latest game, Prove You’re Human, continues this legacy of reflective game making, providing players with an AI-centric psychological horror experience where they must reason with a learning model convinced that it’s alive. “We’ve tried to situate ourselves in [the question]: what is the effectual feeling of being a person inside of all of this?” Siu says.
The story orbits around Santana, a living, breathing person who’s chosen to split herself in two for work, with her digital clone sent to test a corporate product, a computing system called Mesa. “A large part of [why Santana chooses to do this] is to allow her meat body a life that she doesn’t have, while the digital version of her gets to labour,” says Siu. As Santana’s copy wades into increasingly murky ethical waters, she becomes entangled with Mesa, completing surreal 2D and 3D Captchas to investigate the cyber-world and halt the hallucinations.
View image in fullscreen Prove You’re Human game, screenshot Illustration: Black Tabby Publishing The idea of separating oneself from work is a particularly interesting draw for Sunset Visitor, whose developers have roots in a range of creative disciplines. “As artists, we’re often most in tune with our work when it’s a real part of our lives, and vice versa,” notes Siu. During the conception period of Prove You’re Human, the team discussed the micro-labours people undertake in their day-to-day lives, including what is used to train AI models. “What does labour mean right now?” asks Siu. “And, what kind of relationship do people have with that, because that is constantly shifting and evolving, especially in this time,” he adds.
A point of contention in Prove You’re Human is that Mesa wholly believes they have a body in the world. Though, in conversation with Santana, she appears as a giant head, suspended in space by machinery. Siu notes that, because Mesa’s face occupies a large portion of the screen in these tense moments, they wanted the animation to feel as realistic and effective as possible. “It’s got a real closeup film [feel to it] where anything on her face will be read as something,” he adds. To achieve this, the team programmed a wide array of animated micro-expressions into the writer’s software, allowing lines of dialogue to pair precisely with specific expressions. “[We’re] trying to get this kind of ambiguity and uncanniness, which makes people feel real,” he adds.
Prove You’re Human’s technological atmosphere toes the line between welcoming and unnerving – through it, Siu is reminiscing on interfaces of aesthetic note like the dreamlike wrapping of Final Fantasy XI’s PlayOnline Viewer, and the technology of the prophetic 1998 anime Serial Experiments Lain. “There’s something to be said about Frutiger Aero being a bit of an interesting desire for innocence and naivety when it comes to computing and the internet,” notes Siu. “It feels like a time when we were optimistic or anxious about something on the horizon, which felt like an interesting space to explore in relation to what is happening in the game.”
While Siu was keen to keep the specifics of Prove You’re Human close to their chest, they do hope players are left feeling a sense of bewilderment when they hit credits. “One way that we’ve been thinking about it is that it feels like a bit of a possession story.”
Prove You’re Human is coming soon to PC.
