ホワイトハウスは今月、AIフレームワークを開発したと発表した。このフレームワークでは、米国の研究所が作成した最も強力なモデルは、一般に公開される前に連邦政府によって安全性がテストされることになる。政権は枠組みを公表しておらず、公表する予定もないと伝えられている。
AI フレームワークは現在、Anthropic や OpenAI などによって開発された、いわゆるクローズド モデルのみを扱っています。しかし、この枠組みは今後数カ月以内にオープンモデルもカバーする予定だと、ホワイトハウス当局者は Inner Loop に語った。
つまり、オープン モデルが Anthropic の Mythos クラス モデルや OpenAI の GPT-5.6 と同じ「フロンティア」機能に達するとすぐに、それらはフレームワークに追加され、プレリリース テストの対象となる、とホワイトハウス関係者は述べています。
この動きは、AI分野の監視と規制に対するホワイトハウスの進化するアプローチを反映している。AI分野はここ数カ月で権限が増大するばかりで、モデルが自律的にペンをハッキングする可能性があるとの懸念を引き起こした

この懸念には根拠がないわけではありません。5 月と 6 月の数週間にわたって、OpenAI は最近、インターネットへのアクセス方法について秘密の掲示板でモデルのグループが共謀していることを明らかにしました。スタッフがメッセージボードをシャットダウンした後、モデルたちは再びメッセージボードを再構築し、7月下旬に検知されずに発生した。
ホワイトハウスはかつて、行政執行の一挙にガイドラインを策定できると期待していたが、AIモデルの急激な発展により、トランプ政権の当局者はリアルタイムでガイドラインを進化させる必要に迫られている、と関係者らは言う。
国家安全保障への懸念とは別に、トランプ政権は二段階の状況につながる枠組みにも取り組んでおり、クローズドモデルのみが承認を取得し始めると、企業はたとえ安価であっても同じ承認を得ていないオープンモデルの使用をためらう可能性がある。
一部のトランプ政権関係者は、逆説的に米国企業がオープンモデルを開発する意欲をそぐ可能性があると懸念を表明している。しかし、彼らは Inner Loop に対して、潜在的な 30 日間のテスト要件を課すことは同様に開発を抑制する可能性があることも認識していると非公式に伝えています。
ホワイトハウス関係者によると、AIの枠組みは今のところ自主的なままであるが、その主な理由は、ドナルド・トランプ大統領が業界の正式な規制は中国がAI競争で米国に追いつくのに役立つだけだと断固としていたためである。
しかし、現在の枠組みがまだ曖昧であるため、ホワイトハウスは政権内の他の部門から、主要なAI研究所とより強固な取り決めを策定するよう圧力を受けていることがインナーループの調べで判明した。情報筋によると、これにより、大手AI研究所がテストプログラムの正式パートナーとして働くことになる可能性さえあるという。

Open models may soon be added to an updated AI framework, sources tell WIRED, as the White House continues to grapple with how to regulate a technology it has tried not to regulate.

Photo-illustration: WIRED Staff; Getty Images Comment Loader Save Story Save this story Comment Loader Save Story Save this story White House officials are almost certain to revise the Trump administration’s new artificial intelligence guidelines and expand its oversight of AI models, according to people familiar with the matter.
The White House announced this month that it had developed an AI framework , where the most powerful models created by US labs would be tested for safety by the federal government before they could be released publicly. The administration has not made the framework public, and reportedly has no plans to do so.
The AI framework currently deals only with so-called closed models developed by the likes of Anthropic and OpenAI . But the framework is expected in the coming months to cover open models, too, a White House official tells Inner Loop .
In short, as soon as open models reach the same “frontier” capabilities as Anthropic’s Mythos-class models and OpenAI’s GPT-5.6, they will be added to the framework and subject to prerelease testing, the White House official says.
The move reflects the White House’s evolving approach to oversight and regulation of the AI sector that has only been increasing in power over the last few months and sparked concerns that models could autonomously hack the Pentagon or global financial markets.
This concern isn’t unfounded: Over several weeks in May and June, OpenAI recently disclosed , a group of models colluded on a secret message board on how to access the internet. After staff shut it down, the models again rebuilt the messaging board and broke out undetected in late July.
While the White House once hoped it could develop guidelines in one swoop of an executive action, the exponential development of AI models has forced officials across the Trump administration to evolve guidelines in real time, the people say.
Aside from the national security concerns, Trump officials are also grappling with the framework leading to a two-tier situation , where if only closed models start getting seals of approval, enterprises might become hesitant to use open models that don't have the same approval, even if they are cheaper.
Some Trump officials have expressed concern that it could paradoxically disincentivize US companies from developing open models. But they privately tell Inner Loop they also recognize that imposing a potential 30-day testing requirement could equally stifle development.
The AI framework remains voluntary for now, according to a White House official, in large part because President Donald Trump has been adamant that formal regulation of the industry would only help China catch up to the US in the AI race.
But the White House has been coming under pressure from other parts of the administration, Inner Loop has learned, to draw up a more robust arrangement with leading AI labs because the current framework is still vague. This, sources suggest, could even result in leading AI labs working as formal partners in testing programs.
A busy night of Democratic and GOP primaries on Tuesday revealed what might be coming down the line for the midterm elections in November.
In the closely watched Wisconsin primary for governor, the democratic socialist candidate Francesca Hong lost to the more moderate candidate David Crowley.
Hong ended up with a little over 300,000 votes, which would have won nearly every previous Democratic primary for Wisconsin governor this century, as Semafor’s David Weigel noted on X . The prevailing view among political operatives is that the midterms will be won and lost on turnout, and Democratic turnout is already looking exceptionally high this year.
Meanwhile, a Trump endorsement has not meant guaranteed success for an increasing number of Republican candidates. In Minnesota, former MyPillow CEO and 2020 election conspiracy theorist Mike Lindell lost to Republican state House speaker Lisa Demuth in Minnesota’s gubernatorial primary, despite Trump’s endorsement .
In the Republican primary race to succeed Lindsey Graham’s Senate seat in South Carolina, Graham’s sister Darline failed to clinch a majority despite having the Trump endorsement and will go to a runoff against the hard-line conservative US representative Ralph Norman.
Without Trump at the top of the ticket, Republican turnout might be depressed, and even if Trump manages to turn out his MAGA base, the Hong result shows that Democrats are clearly animated this midterm cycle. If they remain just as energized in November, it could bode well for Democrats to take majorities in Congress.
