## Original Extract

A one-button game about navigating advanced technology transitions: hug orphan risks, starve moral panics, deploy tech for good, and keep human flourishing alive.

I'm a professor of advanced technology transitions at ASU, and part of my work includes pushing at the edges of what frontier models can and can't do. Out of curiosity I asked Fable 5, via Claude Code, to research the past two decades of my research and design a browser game from it — something that would stand up as a game on its own, but that genuinely reflected the work. Over 90 iterations later (this was a long collaborative effort) the result was Hyperbubble — a one-button browser-based game where your task is to navigate your character through a future of emerging technologies, complex risks, transformative possibilities, and unexpected delights, all while protecting and growing their state of “flourishing.” Each element of the game tracks back to aspects of my work — even the seemingly weird ones! Sharing as an example of how emerging AI capabilities are allowing complex ideas to be translated into modes and platforms that would have been exceptionally difficult to achieve without them (or prohibitively costly). The game itself is one self-contained HTML file — procedural graphics, synthesized soundtrack, no libraries, no build step, no signup. MIT licensed. The leaderboard is a ~120-line Cloudflare Worker holding five fields per entry and nothing personal: no accounts, no emails, pseudonyms only. Best on desktop or tablet; playing on mobile works but isn't tuned yet, which is the next job. Repo at: https://github.com/2020science/hyperbubble (which also links to more background on the game)

How to play
One button, one bubble, and the whole future off to the right. Everything below is detail — honestly, you could close this now and just play. Come back when something bites you.
Press and hold — the SPACE BAR , the DOWN ARROW , a mouse button, or a finger held anywhere on the screen — and your bubble turns heavy and drops. Let go and it floats back up. That's the entire control scheme. I promise there is no hidden second button.
The trick is to ride the hills the way a skier would: hold on the way down a slope to gather speed, let go just before the bottom, and the next slope throws you into the sky. Land gently on a downhill slope — moving the way it tilts — and you'll hear a chime. Keep landing like that and the chimes climb a scale, your EXUBERANCE multiplier grows (up to ×9.9), and the game quietly starts to sing. This takes about three runs to find, and it's worth all three. A smaller pleasure while you practice: skim just above a hillside without touching it, and the game pays you a little +♥ for the nerve.
One rule worth holding onto: floating and gliding can never hurt you. Diving can — hold all the way into the ground and the bubble cracks. So does a bite from a risk you ignored. Three cracks end the run (five in gentle mode). So when in doubt, do nothing. Surprisingly good advice, in the game and in general.
The YEAR is how far you've come — it's the score. The pink bar is HUMAN FLOURISHING , and it's really the whole game: it leaks away as the pace of change rises, and it refills when you look after the things you meet (watch for the +♥ signs). If it empties, the lights go out. Push it past 100 and the future starts blooming — literally: flowers appear, and every flower you ever grow joins the meadow on the title screen, forever.
PACE only ever climbs on its own. That's not a flaw — that's the thesis. But you can push back: hold SHIFT , or rest two fingers on the screen, and the world slows down on purpose — the kind of pause people take together when a technology is moving faster than the questions. You'll see it: SLOWING DOWN TO THINK spreads across the sky. Time slows so you can look around, the pace of change genuinely drops and stays dropped for a good while, the hype cools off, and the things that bite bite softer — because this time, you saw them coming. It costs flourishing the whole time you hold it, because thinking time isn't free. It's still the best deal in the game.
And mind the dashed line high in the sky — the hype line. Soar above it and the bubble inflates: bigger, floatier, more fragile, and flourishing drains fast up there. Stay too long and it pops, and nothing will have burst the bubble but the bubble.
The wide-eyed blobs are orphan risks — risks nobody has claimed yet. Hold your button as you roll past one and you adopt it: it gets a name, trails behind you like a duckling, and will one day take a hit meant for you. Fly past it instead and it burrows underground... then returns a few hills later with teeth. That's a bite : it knocks the bubble like a crash, takes a real chunk of flourishing, and the end-of-run card will tell you exactly what it took: trust, dignity, the quiet. From era 4, some blobs carry cameras or antennas — nastier than the plain ones if ignored, but adopt them and the paparazzo's flash starts defending you, and the trending one lends you a permanent tailwind. Keep eight pets, and adopting a ninth graduates your eldest, with a gift.
The scribbly fires are moral panics, and attention is their fuel. Touch one and it flares up and comes after you. Fly past without touching and it dies down on its own — and flourishing grows a little. Three of them marching together is a mob. Never hug a mob.
The diamond signs are risks with names — DEEPFAKE SCANDAL, DATA BREACH, and friends. Hold your button at the moment you hit one and you've managed it (+♥). Coast into it slack-thumbed and it costs you dearly. Or fly over it entirely, if you can.
The crates are emerging technologies. Catch one and a five-second timer starts; when it runs out, the technology deploys wherever you happen to be — so where you are is the decision . On the ground: tech for good — flowers, +♥♥. Above the hype line: RUSHED TO MARKET — a burst of speed and a year gained now, new problems hatching downstream. Some of these technologies are real, some are almost real, and some are nonsense. I'm not telling you which is which. Being unsure is the point.
The golden ? is serendipity. Touch it and something good happens. Never the same thing twice.
The grey sludge pools in the deepest valleys, drags at your speed, and eats flourishing while you wade. Build speed and fly over the top of it.
And from era 5, the black swans : two honks, and then it's on you. A pet will throw itself in the way, if you have one. Pass close by without touching, and the near miss pays two full years.
Medals arrive at 10, 25, 50, 100, and 250 hills: DISRUPTION, AWARENESS, AGILITY, RESILIENCE, DIGNITY. The last one is worth seeing.
Survive long enough and the world itself turns — the colors, the board, the doodles, the music — through THE AGE OF DEEP WATER, THE LONG DUSK, and THE AGE OF STRANGE LIGHTS. Past year 3000, arrive at any century still flourishing and a door opens to the post-scarcity future: the drain stops, the panics retire, and only the ground still counts. Records earned there wear ✿. And the story does have an ending — a good one, at year 4000. Bring everything you have grown.
Press T (or tap the purple band on the title screen) and you'll play TODAY'S FUTURE — the identical future everyone in the world is playing today, under the same name. Your daily best is kept separately, and the end-of-run card grows a copy chip, for comparing results with people you enjoy arguing with.
field notes is the bestiary of everything you've met so far. the ideas behind this is where the game came from. sound is sound. gentle mode ☁ makes the whole game kinder — built for younger players, and there's no shame in it. marginalia ✎ lets the professor's stray thoughts drift by mid-run; catching one earns a sparkle, a chime, and absolutely nothing else — which is somehow still the best trade in the game. And the board , top-center, is the worldwide leaderboard — pseudonyms only, and a run is posted only when you choose to, from the end-of-run card. One warning that matters: press-and-hold your best-year line and the game starts forgetting everything . It shows you a progress bar so you have time to change your mind.

The world has lately grown more small things, and it would be wrong to list them all. But: the weather is occasionally sheep. Or kites, going the wrong way. It varies. There is a playground every century or three, always built a little differently, because there is always a playground. An orange tree grows a long way from home, and once in a while there's a jar. A dish listens. A reader turns the page only if you pass slowly enough to deserve it. A tower on a hill is passing a name on, and always will be. The duck, it turns out, remembers you — across runs, out of order. And somewhere out there is a photographer nobody ever sees, whose photographs turn up later, where the flowers are.
This generation of the game also keeps a small pool of wonders — a bench, a cold pool, a man in a white suit, an elephant made of leaves, and a handful more. At most one appears per run, and most runs carry none, so meeting one means something. The weather has private moods too. And if you do nothing at all for twelve seconds, the game notices, kindly. HYPERBUBBLE is a one-button game that's inspired by navigating powerful new technologies in a complex world while ensuring future human flourishing. It's designed to be simple to play while sparking curiosity, experimentation and wonder (and you'll need all three to get the most out of it). Devised and coded by Claude Fable 5, and based on the work, thinking, and initiatives of ASU Professor of Advanced Technology Transitions Andrew Maynard.
A quick note on the game's genesis from Andrew Maynard
HYPERBUBBLE started as an idle moment of speculation while I was exploring Anthropic's Fable 5
AI model. I was in between more complex (and serious) projects, and maximizing use of Fable before
my access ran out. Wanting to test its capabilities in coding a simple web-based game, I asked it to
research my work and come up with something inspired by it that was simple, fun, and with gameplay
that kept you coming back.
HYPERBUBBLE is the result. In some ways it's nothing special — the world is overflowing with
simple AI-generated games. What was interesting to me though was the process, and how the game taps
into my work in very specific ways. It also provides some insight into how Fable 5 works. Plus, I
must confess to loving the game!
After my initial request, Fable did a deep research dive into my work, created five game-designer
agents to come up with different concepts, passed these through a panel of three AI judges, and
selected the winning (AI-generated) proposal.
After the first draft — which Fable debugged, tested, and checked using an impressive array of
sub-agents — it began working earnestly with me to come up with the final product, refining the
goals, gameplay, and feel along the way. This resulted in version 1.0 of the game and represented
over 20 iterations.
Version 1.0 was originally published on July 10 and featured on the
Future of Being Human Substack . However, not content to leave
things as they were, I then started to work in earnest with Fable on a version 2.0 that expanded the
gameplay substantially, and the ideas and experiences that a player encounters, and then progressed to a version 3.0!
The game is now on version 5 and is considerably more mature than that first iteration, even down to having a leaderboard.
It represents over 90 iterations of the initial html file that Fable
produced, and has a far greater steer from me than version 1.0 did. However, the fundamentals and
implementation still belong to the sometimes-quirky and often unexpected "creative mind" of
Fable 5.
That said, the game is intentionally simple. And there are many aspects of it that are still
begging to be tweaked and refined. But it still brings a smile to my face every time I play :)
