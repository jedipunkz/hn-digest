It's a worthwhile question, but it's badly formed: am I a better what ? And even that construction is missing a grander idea: are we better, measurably, because of AI? If so, how? Why? How does AI change us?
The reason one might ask if I am better is because I think my output as a writer and a programmer has improved, largely because the barriers that gate my cadence - good interlocution without prior biases and, well, time - have changed. I can ask silly questions, knowing they're silly, without having to seed the environment so my flights of fancy aren't seen as utterly random - because they're not, but they seem like it 1 . And I can loop far faster with an AI than I can with the "real world" because the AI can construct knowledge structures faster than I can code them to try them out.
But "better" here is still an impression - and take away the AI and the barriers to cadence return, and thus "am I better" turns into "yes, as long as the tools remain" - which is probably true of any craftsman. A man who can build a ship on his own given a full workshop is not going to be as productive when you replace the workshop with a single adze. A Java programmer's not going to be all that grand at Java when all he has is a C compiler and /bin/cat to generate programs with.
The problem here is that there's no counterfactual. I can't go back in time, even to three years ago, and see what I would be like today had I snorted and sniffed and yanked my braid 2 at the idea of using tools that I think are pretty ordinary today.
Even so, I think I can look at the satisfaction I have in my own output, and the responses of others to that output: I use AI, and people accuse me of using AI, but they don't point at the things that would give it away if I used AI poorly. They point at the quality , as if good quality were now a marker of manufacture.
I create faster than I did; I write as if I had an interested partner pushing back on the worst of my impulses. Sure, my "partner" wants to sand down virtually everything that makes me who I am as a writer 3 , but the fact that there's a stream to push against gives me better direction than I'd have had otherwise: the AI can infer how to push my buttons, and the right buttons, better than most humans can, partly because I can tell it about my buttons without it going "... is this really who you are?"

I interact with AIs fairly consistently and constantly. I don't ask the AIs to do my primary analysis for me, and in fact I warn them not to - when I ask about something I think is interesting, I want it to form context before I say what I think; I don't want the LLM looking for confirmation of what I think on first reads.
I actually struggle with that process some, even now. I think I have something that works well, but there's still the shadow of something better somewhere, I just don't know what it looks like yet. Even so, here's the way this practice works out: say there's an interesting article I wish to summarize or understand. What I'll do is read the article myself to form my first impressions, to decide whether I think it's possibly of interest enough to keep going; if I think there's something there, then what I'll do is submit it to an AI for summarization and analysis, without my observations, with a warning that my observations are coming up after context is loaded.
Then, the AI summarizes as directed, and then I say why I thought it was interesting or relevant. This is synthesis: the LLMs have the words, I have my own perspective, and we end up affecting each other; sometimes I'm way off base, sometimes I'm seeing the Thing Behind The Thing. This is where the special outcomes live; if I'm way off base, I learn, and if I have insight, I get to work out how that insight works and we might have something worth reading as a result.
And all along the way, I'm horribly polite, because I want to be someone for whom politesse is a default setting. I don't chastise the machine seriously (I will make jokes to it as I would to myself, but that's it: there's no malice involved.)
So after all that: am I better because of AI? Again, better at what ? I still can't tell you, and I've been circling "why" this whole article. What I can tell you is what the machine enables me to do: a model has no real exposure to me, it gets what I write down and nothing else. So I wrote myself down: how I judge, what I refuse, what I value, what I think my flaws are. That's an aggregation of my entire body of writing over decades, distilled into a few pages, written not for readers but as a summary for a thing that cannot work with what it cannot know.
Writing it down doesn't improve anything. But writing it down gives both me and the LLM a chance to examine what I think is important, and work with that. Writing it down helps me be legible to myself , and... I think becoming legible is being better. The world works better when we understand more, even if we understand only ourselves.
