Brave sells ads, which are anonymous (unlike google's adsense ads) and conform to its own (fairly stringent) privacy practices, and which appear throughout its results. If you would prefer not to see ads at all (and still want to support brave search) you can subscribe for $3 a month or $30 a year, which removes all of brave's ads. However, you must use the brave browser for the ad-free subscription option. Brave is a good choice if you want a free search with its own index, and a robust privacy policy. Its results are largely in line with DDG. Brave has "AI" features, but they can be turned off easily in the settings.
Qwant is a french search engine now used as the default by the UK parliament, as of 06/04/26, as the UK and greater Europe increasingly wean themselves off of google search. Qwant has its own indexing system like Kagi and Brave, which is the European Search Perspective or EUSP, a joint index created by Qwant, in France, and Ecosia, in Germany. Qwant is focused on privacy, does not track users, resell personal data, or bias the display of search results, according to their wikipedia entry. Qwant has some AI-related features, but they can be disabled.
Ecosia is a German search engine thats main hook is being an eco-friendly search engine. It's powered by renewable energy sources and uses some portion of its profits (I am not sure what percentage exactly, but it seems to be about 1/4 of its total income?) on planting trees along with some other eco-friendly programs. That being said it does have a whole news post entitled " Our AI just got even better," the byline of which is: "We're building AI that is different. It answers to the planet, not Big Tech. And it's now more European - which means more independent, and more private." So... yeah. It also uses both google and bing for results in addition to the the EUSP index that it partners with Qwant for. So you may end up using google on the backend anyway. You can turn off AI overview in the settings . I guess using Ecosia comes down to how granola you are and how much you trust their eco-marketing. Technically, still not actually google, though.
Some of the old engines still exist, sort of:
Dogpile.com / Metacrawler.com / Webcrawler.com / startpage.com are all owned and operated by System1, an "internet advertising company." Upsides? None of them are google, and none of them have AI features (though startpage's settings includes links to vanish.ai, a private AI model). Downsides? It's an internet advertising company . All of these are "meta" searches, in that they use the indexes of multiple search engines, including google and bing. I think I would use DDG or Brave if you need a free search engine. Still, these aren't google, and metacrawlers return the top results from multiple engines at once, so if you don't want to use DDG, Kagi, or Brave, you could do worse. Probably.
