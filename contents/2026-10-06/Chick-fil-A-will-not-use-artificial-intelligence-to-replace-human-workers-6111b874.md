Chick-fil-A will not use artificial intelligence to replace human workers in the drive-thru
Chick-fil-A CEO Andrew Cathy told CNBC that the company wants to keep human hospitality at the heart of its drive-thru experience ... 'From our experience, we really want that hospitality to be human to human,' Cathy said. 'We're not going to substitute that interaction with technology, because we feel like that hospitality is so important to create that warm environment for consumers.'
Chatbots in the drive-thru are becoming more of the norm as restaurants look to suck the soul out of food service and treat us more like cattle save money.
Major fast-food competitors are moving fast toward automated order taking. McDonald's plans to test its voice AI technology, 'Archy,' in English and Spanish, while Wendy's is deploying its Google Cloud-based 'FreshAi' system.
You know how else those chains are using AI? 👇
Imagine the future: With the Flock cameras integrated into the drive-thru, McDonald's Archy runs a statistical analysis to determine you are a fast-food addict who will pay up to 40% more for a Big Mac. It uses facial and vehicle recognition to connect you via the app and changes the digital drive-thru board to display your personalized, more expensive prices.
Archy then tries to upsell you using highly specific psychological algorithms. Maybe McDonald's even installs scent machines to hit you with the smell of your favorite order to boost dopamine and serotonin levels and make you more prone to buy more!
You then pay 50% more than the car in front of you, pull forward, pay the automated cashier, and have one lone human worker deliver the food (he'll be replaced within the decade).
Chick-fil-A is taking a different approach.
Despite rejecting voice AI at the speaker post, Chick-fil-A still uses non-voice technology to support its workers. In 2024, the company opened an elevated drive-thru in McDonough, Georgia, featuring a four-lane layout and a conveyor system that delivers meals to workers every six seconds.
Human minds using their Imago Dei to develop machines that help teams of image-bearers thrive.
That's the way God's Chicken does it!
