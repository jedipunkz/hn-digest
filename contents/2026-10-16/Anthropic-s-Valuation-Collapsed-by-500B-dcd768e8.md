Telegraph Reporters
Published
16 October 2026 2:42am BST
Anthropic investors are considering slashing the company’s ‌valuation by $500bn (£379bn) after its latest accounts revealed slowing growth, according to a report.
Some investors who attended Anthropic’s investor day on Wednesday in San Francisco discussed a valuation of about $1.5tn, Reuters reported , a significant step down from the $2tn that had previously been expected for the November listing.
At that meeting, Anthropic announced revenue of nearly $17bn as it reportedly turned a profit last quarter.
But investors were reported to be disappointed by the tech giant’s slowing ​growth rate and the inflated costs of AI infrastructure.
Its $17bn revenues were reportedly up around 45pc compared to the $11.6bn recorded in the second quarter. However, that was a slowdown from 146pc growth recorded between the first and second quarters.
Investor jitters come amid broader concerns over the ballooning cost of financing AI and a tech sell-off that has swept Wall Street and Asian markets in recent days.
The tech giant told investors on Wednesday that its revenue had more than trebled since the first quarter of 2026, which was first reported by the Financial Times.
A cooler reception for Anthropic’s initial public offering (IPO) could weigh on the wider AI market, whose near-term prospects investors see as hinging largely on the share sale’s success, followed by OpenAI’s potential listing next ​year.
Anthropic declined to comment.
The global tech sell-off is roiling financial markets and in turn, hurting AI infrastructure companies, chipmakers and “​neocloud” providers that rent computing power for AI broadly.
A disappointing share sale for Anthropic could further add to investor concerns about the profitability of AI.
The company’s most prominent investors attended Wednesday’s meeting and met Dario Amodei, Anthropic’s chief executive, and Krishna Rao, its finance chief.
Founded in 2021 by ​former OpenAI executives, including Mr Amodei and ⁠his sister Daniela Amodei who is now Anthropic’s president, Anthropic has emerged as one of the leading generative AI developers with its Claude family of models.
The company’s explosive growth has been underpinned by Alphabet’s Google ​and Amazon, which are simultaneously investors, suppliers of computing power and important distribution partners, as well as ​AI competitors.
In its IPO prospectus, Anthropic laid out a vision that AI would transform the ⁠global economy ​more profoundly than industrialisation, electricity and the internet, even as it reported a loss of $42bn in 2025 and projected more costs.
The company has committed to spending at least $518bn over a decade for cloud services, computing and infrastructure.
