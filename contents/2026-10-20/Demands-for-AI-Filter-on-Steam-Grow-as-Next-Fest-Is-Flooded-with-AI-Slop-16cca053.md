Demands for AI Filter on Steam Grow as Next Fest Is Flooded with AI Slop
The October 2026 edition of Steam Next Fest started on October 19, allowing users to explore Steam’s massive catalog of games with free playable demos. Millions of PC gamers logged on but only to find the entire event plagued by… you guessed it.. AI slop. An event that was historically known to showcase hidden gems is now devolved into a showcase of cheap AI made games.
Seeing how bad things this year are, demands from PC gamers for a global “no-AI filter” for Steam are growing across X, Reddit , Discord, and other social media platforms. Gamers voicing intense frustration over an influx of low-effort, AI-generated titles all over Next Fest and Steam in general.
The easiest solution right now would be an AI filter.
Earlier in the year, Valve updated its Steamworks content policy to mandate that developers explicitly disclose the use of generative AI (across pre-generated art, code, text, or live voice assets) during store registration. However, there are games even in the AAA and AA space that hide their use of AI generated assets, text, voice etc.
I reported on Tainted Grail and RideShare Stimulator earlier this year; two games that hid their use of AI on Steam. It won’t be surprising at all if more game start to hide AI use to avoid getting caught in the Steam “no-AI filter”, when and if Valve adds one.
For now, for players browsing the thousands of Next Fest demos, finding genuine, hand-crafted games has become an exhausting chore. On the other hand, indie devs are afraid of AI-generated copycat games and some go as far as pulling out of Steam Next Fest to avoid getting their game stolen.
In a stark example of developer fallout right before Next Fest, publisher Alliance Arts and developer “WHO YOU” officially canceled the public Steam Next Fest demo for “Drunken Goddess Reflux”.
The developer decided it is best to hold a closed playtest rather than put the game on Steam Next Fest in order to protect their build. AI slopers use tools to quickly scrape, replicate, and launch clones even before original games come out.
Valve is yet to add an AI filter for Steam but if you’re looking for an alternative immediately, I recommend using “AI warning for Steam” which is an open source browser extension.
AI tools have ended the barrier to entry for rapid asset flips and source code theft. The least Steam can do right now is add a “no-AI filter” until more permanent solutions are figured out.
