## Original Extract

Paolo Belcastro Cynical Optimist
Five AI assistants, first impressions
I’ve been testing Grok Bot and Instinct for a while. As I was recently in the US for a couple of weeks, I included Dots and Muse in the test. This morning I added Wabi . These are my first impressions.
Instinct is the most approachable. There’s no separate app: you use it through Messages or WhatsApp. After a while, this quality turns into a problem, because it’s hard to keep track of many parallel conversations. If you are just getting your feet wet, it’s a good start.
Dots immediately blew my mind by being proactive and correctly handling complex interactions. The problem is, it disappeared the moment I landed in Europe, which instantly eliminates it from my day-to-day. Try it if you never come to the EU.
Muse is super polished, has a nice personality, and is very upfront. The biggest issue is that its candor has so far mostly been used to “give it to me straight” (whatever that means) when it can’t do things. Underwhelming overall.
Wabi I just installed, so this is very early. It very aggressively wants to help proactively, which is a quality in my book. The UI is polished and very pleasant to use. So far so good; we’ll see how it goes, but I’m positive.
I could rank these four, but it would be subjective. Which one works for you depends on where you live and what you’re looking for in terms of interface and task complexity.
Grok Bot isn’t playing in a different league; it’s playing a whole different game. It works everywhere, in great native apps, has a ton of connectors, and lets you run multiple agents that talk to each other. It’s not an assistant, it’s your own personal company. A game changer.
I have used Claude Code as a GBrain driver for months, and I’m very happy with the setup, but opening my brain to Grok Bot over MCP unleashed its true power. I used it to migrate my sites to Spacefast , and now it maintains them.
For the first time, I have assistants working for me 24/7.
