YEAR 1, FALL 2025-2026
The Three-Layer Cake
Public-Interest Information in an AI-Intermediated Age
This report brings together what we learned in our first year of convenings and conversations with journalists, technologists, funders and others. It starts from the idea that news will be intermediated by AI, whether we like it or not, and maps the information ecosystem in three layers: consumption, synthesis and creation. It lays out where newsrooms, technologists, funders and allies outside journalism can act while the new infrastructure of information is still being built.
Readers will increasingly dictate what information they want to receive. The news industry can resist that, or learn to serve it with a public-service lens.
This world could magnify misinformation, manipulation and polarization. It could also help underserved communities find news and information that speaks to and for them.
AI agents that collect and compile news and information need to work in the best interests of their users, not other masters.
Better digital infrastructure and standards are critical, so that information can travel with context, nuance and provenance.
Understanding what people care about and want to know will be one of the great prizes of the AI age. Who should own that data?
There are strategies that newsrooms can follow to survive and even thrive in this new environment.
But the most useful interventions require collective action and live in the infrastructure of the ecosystem, not in newsrooms.
And we have a limited amount of time to help shape the future of news.
