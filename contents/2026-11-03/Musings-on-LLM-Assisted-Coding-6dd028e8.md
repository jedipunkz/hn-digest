Musings on LLM-assisted coding
I consider myself a "indie" developer ever since I've decided to take on
Livtet more seriously. It's become both a project of passion and something
to sharpen my learnings as I continue to look for work . I think anyone can
consider this label as they work on things for the public that they intend to
have some sort of audience consume or take on. But it's not just being a
"software developer" anymore. Thanks to the advent of LLMs, folks can do what
used to take days in hours [1] . Speed used to be a defining factor of how folks
in this speed produced applications. Yes , taste, design and affects all matter
— however if your competitor or rival was able to release more features
and design updates quicker than you can, your analytics can show you what's
become apparent: users can and will drop off [2] .
This is to say, skilled development is possible and has been possible with
LLMs for some time . I'm realizing that this is a bit of a controversial thing
to say. Because of a near-allergic reaction folks might have with the
technology that they already have been using to some capacity (be it through
Google's search through the company's own admission), no one is actually trying :
to see if there's options beyond the Big Three
how these things actually work
what and why frontier models operate
This works in favor of the companies that folks are allegedly against because
it allows them to solely control the narrative on how these systems work. I
do not believe that they'll be capable of replacing entire engineering teams
but I have a bias as an engineer to believe that my work - knowledge work - is
unique and cemented enough that nothing can surpass it. At best, we'll see
(and already have) more labor reductions as more work is transitioned from
conventional software engineering into what Laurie Voss called product
engineering . If you can have a machine spit out robust enough code - which
is what we already expected from mid-level developers and below to produce -
then we can have the more talented and experienced developers shave off rough
edges in an automated fashion and focus on business (or non-business, I'm not
a MBA) value.
What does that mean for indies?
This is either a golden period for independent developers or a bleak point,
depending on how you choose to operate.
To be a AI vegan is going to require a dramatic upheaval of how one operates.
You'll have to forego using Windows, Linux or macOS since each of these
operating systems deploy code generated by LLMs [3] . You'll have to find new
hosting providers that don't provide any sort of AI compute. You could be more
of a ovo-vegetarian and stick to places like DigitalOcean or Cloudflare while
running your human-grade software.
If you're more reasonable (or at the very least are doing the same thing when
React took over the Web, or when Amazon's approach to Web development infected
every aspect of the industry), you're probably looking at tools that aren't run
by the Big Three (Cursor, Claude Code or Codex) because you want more control
over how your harness works and are looking for something akin to how a game
developer writes their own map editing software over time (except that you're
mainly adjusting configuration and what models you're making use of).
Yes. I use LLMs daily for my software development. I've already written about
my initial angst and then acceptance about them on here. They're not capable of
doing anything by themselves because they're call and response machines but
folks are working to make them more automated and networked.
In my own use case, I'm having it run across pages on
https://livtet.olamaelcu.net/ and monitor reporting logs from Sentry to find
bugs, file them on GitHub and have other agents do the work of picking them up
and closing them with evidence videos and screenshots to prevent regressions.
This would have normally been a few days' of effort for a gnarly bug. Now it's
automated away in a few hours. And for someone like me who's trying to build
something that can compete with https://fable.co/ or https://margins.app/ ,
I'm willing to take on all of the help I can get.
All in all, I've accepted this new wave of development. The same way I accepted:
React as the defacto means of frontend framework development despite being
not a fan
English as the primary language of software development despite not only
speaking it
12 Factor development, which was a decent and good idea actually
Datadog/Cloudflare over Sentry for application telemetry (Sentry's open
enough to run at home)
I believe a level of knowledge work crisis of identity is happening for
software developers and it's only a matter of time before we actually reckon
with it. And when folks are ready, there's a few books [4] folks should sit
down and study before making another move.
See https://blacksky.community/profile/did:plc:e2ctbutx6kya6si4if5ngjmm/post/3mz72mo72fk24 's quoted post. ↩︎
There's always exceptions to rules and theories. Loyal customers tend not
to be swayed so easily by things like this. Data moats and the labor of
migration are conventionally what keeps people at bay and resistant to leaving.
I would be curious to see, if this was less of the tiding force, how things
would play. ATProto provides this as a feature. ↩︎
I'm assuming it to be the case for macOS because they tend to be industry
adopters of technology but they have a high bar of code quality so the code
generated has to at least pass that (which is more than one can say for other
projects). ↩︎
