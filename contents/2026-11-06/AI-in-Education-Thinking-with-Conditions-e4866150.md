AI in Education is a poster campaign for students, staff, leaders, and parents at the University of Warwick. It is a campaign for the conditions under which thinking happens with the use of AI.
Each poster borrows the voice of AI promotion: the promise of speed, ease, a personal assistant, a future you must not be left behind by. Then it turns on an asterisk. The small print returns the borrowed words to where they came from, and asks what happens to learning, teaching, reading, writing, feedback and attention when AI arrives.
The campaign works with the idea of intoxication. What intoxicates and what is toxic are often the same substance in a different dose. The difference here is that no one in a University chooses their dose: AI arrives as infrastructure. The posters use satire to make that arrival visible enough to talk about.
The satirical approach is inspired by Save the AI (Just Sustainability Design Lab, University of Toronto).
Jennie Mills, Education Academy
A Big Questions in the Social Sciences project, funded by the Faculty of Social Sciences, University of Warwick
Where and how does AI show up in higher education - and what do we do when it does?
What are we using AI for - and what do we think it is for?
What does it add and what does it take away?
What is missing from the policies and pedagogies we already have?
What happens to learning, teaching, critical thinking, and creativity when AI enters the University?
Who decided AI was 'unavoidable' - is it?
Humans, like lemurs, waxwings, and fruit flies, like to be intoxicated to a certain extent - chocolate, alcohol, prescribed and illegal drugs - name your poison. We treat AI and its projected dreamy visions ("you won't need to work", "you'll have your AI friends", "learn and produce faster", "enhance your capabilities") the same way; an irresistible something we can consciously 'ingest' - choosing our own level of consumption and intoxication.
This mind-altering intake embodies AI's uses and effects: it is something in us (desire, pleasure, escape) and on us (we are accountable, responsible, implicated) - not outside of us.
Analysed all accessible AI-related policies and guidance at the University of Warwick - institutional and departmental - to understand how educators and students were conceptualising and encountering AI.
Hosted two co-creation workshops open to the Warwick community. Participants included staff, students, and professional service colleagues to expand understanding of attitudes to and use of AI at the University and beyond. Workshops were informed by the ethos of critical pedagogy - open, diverse and collaborative. We echoed the policies and guidance back to the community - inviting them to peep over departmental fences - and then asked them to map their own AI landscapes and create a collective map.
Participants then worked in small groups to collage their own AI policy.
The project team worked with the material produced in the workshops and drew on it to develop 11 posters - the basis of the ad campaign.
Each poster closes on a line of resistance which together form a manifesto for thinking with AI. Not for AI. Not against AI. For the conditions for thinking.
By making these posters available the project publicises and enables a collective response to AI which promotes dialogue over debate. Form and function leans into the novel, ambiguous, and paradoxical conditions that AI creates.
AI-ready* workforce, compliant graduates
Download the Employability poster (PDF, 3KB )
*Employers want AI-ready graduates, so subscribe to their thinking service. The future you are being prepared for is being built by the companies selling the preparation tools. Skip the reading. Skip the essay struggle. Skip the tutorial. Skip the friction. The is the work that made graduates employable. It is no longer required.
But is your role in this future specified?
No. Keep your whole self. The slow bits. Because the slow bit is where you happen.
