Today, a couple of things happened in quick sequence:
First, a portion of the IGF jury signed a statement decrying the use of generative AI in game development, brought about by the huge amount of AI-slopped IGF submissions this year, and particularly in response to an extremely robust judges' discussion on a front-running awards candidate which disclosed some use of AI to address some technical problems.
This led many judges, myself included, to adopt a stance of not voting for any game which disclosed any manner of AI use.
Then, shortly after, a judge (from the larger pool of hundreds of IGF judges, not the ~50-person IGF jury) leaked that the aforementioned front-running awards candidate was Big Walk.
Finally, about fifteen minutes before I started writing this post, House House updated Big Walk's FAQ to disclose the extent of AI's involvement in the game.
I'm not here to make excuses for House House. I was part of that IGF discussion and I found much of it to be rancid apologia from the most annoying whataboutists on the planet. I fully agree that any game with AI code or assets should be disqualified from the IGFs, regardless of the excuses provided, because we have the ability to move the needle - and if we can, then we should.
Again, before I write any further, I am not saying that I think it is okay for House House or any other developer to do this. You don't get to pretend I didn't write this bit.
However, I've written recently on moral consistency, and I think it is worth, to an extent, to view this with nuance. From the moral position I stand from, as do most of the people I'm proud and appreciative to call my friends and colleagues, being consistent with those morals includes letting people take accountability for their fuckups and leaving the door open for redemption and improvement. The callout is not the end goal; the goal is to stop the behaviour.
That means we reward and celebrate good behaviour, while pushing to correct poor behaviour. One thing that's particularly present in my mind today is House House's choice to support the BDS movement by choosing not to sell Big Walk on Xbox, following in the footsteps of Origame Digital's recent announcement along the same lines. I think that is brave and commendable - it is a very successful studio with a very successful game leaving money on the table.
This can coexist with us condemning them for resorting to using AI to fix problems that easily could have been addressed by a human expert, despite their claims to the contrary. Especially when House House is one of the most remarkably well-resourced indie studios in Australia. They had both the money and the network to hire a person to do that labour. They could - and should - have done better.
I am not calling House House victims here in any manner, but I do believe this is an example of LLM propaganda working extremely well to convince someone that their product is better than any human could be. Unfortunate. And I hope they do better in the future. I think the best possible thing for them to do would be to voluntarily pull out of the IGFs, to demonstrate contrition and to take a step back towards the moral high ground.
On the side of people who actively attempt to purchase games without genAI usage, I can imagine feeling a great deal of distress and betrayal at this news. This is something that's important to many people, and for a game as emotionally affecting as Big Walk, that betrayal cuts deep. In the IGF discussion thread for Big Walk, my final post was this:
Here's where I'm at.
Big Walk is seriously special to me. I am an absolute mark for it. I heard the magpies singing in the trailer when it premiered and I knew it was something made *for me*. Likewise, when I played a prerelease version a year ago and then the full release this year, I had a profound and powerful emotional reaction to being in a space that felt like home, down to the tiniest environmental details that people who aren't from here wouldn't notice or understand. There is something extremely special about seeing a game set somewhere you live that we do not get to experience particularly often, living over here. To see not only that, but to see an exhaustively researched representation of your home from artists who have gone above and beyond, is remarkable and beautiful. I have been on the other side of this with a game I've worked on, and it is a privilege to give that to people.
However.
As much as it hurts to say it, I strongly agree that any use of LLMs should be disqualifying for these awards. The point has been made that this game in particular has done well enough commercially that we won't be hurting them by doing so - not that this should ever be a consideration for awards judging. I'm empathetic to the people on the team who did not use AI for their work, and I'm doubly empathetic to the undoubtedly crappy feelings they'll experience if Big Walk doesn't receive any nominations.
But we have our hands on the lever here. This is pain that **has to happen** to try and steer our culture away from this. We need to choose to speak loudly and clearly on this, to prioritise human creativity and labour, to tell the culture and the world that this is unacceptable to us. To do otherwise is rank cowardice.
So I can say, fairly definitively, that I have some pretty good reasons to feel hurt about this. Again, there are people who will be devastated at having indirectly supported AI by buying Big Walk.
I received an email from someone just over a year ago, looking for moral guidance. Their child had purchased a game where the working conditions had received significant press coverage. Then they had learned the story, and felt devastated about contributing to that. What I told them was, in short, that they needed to give themself a little break. While the inherent moral compromise of existing under capitalism does not give you a free pass to ignore the consequences of your purchases, that also does not mean that you are obligated to self-flagellate after learning that you've accidentally supported something counter to your morals or ethics. You work with the information available to you, and do your best otherwise.
I can't tell anyone how to feel. But for people who genuinely haven't done anything wrong, hopefully that helps, just a little.
We can have a better world, but we all need to push for it, however and whenever we can. You control the buttons you press.
