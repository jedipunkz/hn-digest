Anthropic , the AI company behind the Claude chatbot, has been spending like a company that needs friends in Washington. Federal lobbying disclosures show it paid $740,000 to Continental Strategy, a firm run in Washington by Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s former Senate chief of staff.
That firm also counts the daughter of White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles as a partner. In a city built on relationships, that is about as well connected as a lobbying shop gets.
Continental Strategy is one of two Trump-connected firms on Anthropic’s payroll. The other is Ballard Partners, the firm Wiles previously worked at. Together, the two firms received $1.63 million from Anthropic to lobby offices inside the Trump administration, according to the disclosures.
The $740,000 paid to Continental Strategy covers the period through September 2026. Anthropic brought Ballard Partners on board in April 2026.
That hiring date is worth noticing. It came after disputes broke out between Anthropic and the Pentagon over supply chain risks tied to AI applications.
According to the disclosures, Anthropic’s lobbying agenda covers a cluster of national security topics. These include:
AI procurement by the federal government
Defense policy and national security applications of AI
Acceptable use restrictions governing how AI can be deployed
That last item is the crux of the friction. Anthropic and the Trump administration, along with the Pentagon, have been at odds over what counts as acceptable use of AI in defense and surveillance settings.
A lobbying budget that keeps multiplying
The Rubio- and Wiles-linked contracts sit inside a much larger spending surge. Anthropic’s total federal lobbying spend has climbed sharply over three years.

The figures from the disclosures:
The $1.63 million routed to the two Trump-connected firms represents a meaningful slice of the 2026 total. It also shows where Anthropic thinks the leverage sits: the executive branch, not just Congress.
That is a tactical choice. Procurement decisions, acceptable use guidance and supply chain risk designations largely come from agencies, not from floor votes. If your problem lives in the Pentagon and the State Department, you hire people who know the hallways there.
Why the AI industry is lawyering up in Washington
Anthropic is not alone in treating Washington as a growth market for its lobbying budget. AI companies broadly are preparing for regulatory frameworks that have yet to arrive, and AI has become a focal point of legislative scrutiny under the current administration.
What makes Anthropic’s situation distinct is the open dispute with defense officials. Most AI firms lobbying today are trying to influence future rules. Anthropic is also trying to resolve a live disagreement about how its technology may be used by the military and in surveillance contexts.
What this means for Anthropic and its rivals
For Anthropic, the most immediate question is whether the spending buys a resolution with the Pentagon. The company’s lobbying priorities, especially acceptable use restrictions and procurement, map directly onto its dispute with defense officials.
There is also a reputational dimension. Anthropic has built its brand around a careful, safety-focused approach to AI. Paying firms tied to the administration’s most powerful insiders is a pragmatic move, but it puts the company squarely in the same influence game as everyone else in Washington.
The next round of federal lobbying disclosures will show whether Anthropic’s 2026 spending keeps climbing past $8.11 million. Any shift in Pentagon guidance on acceptable use or supply chain risk for AI vendors would indicate whether the effort is working.
And the Ballard Partners relationship, which began only in April 2026, will show whether hiring Wiles’ former firm translates into access at the White House level.
