In this paper, we attempt this measurement. For clarity, we begin by defining four key terms. First, “LLM output tokens” refers to total tokens processed by LLMs minus input and cached tokens. Second, “indexed public web” refers to text on public websites that can be found on a search engine results page. Third, “private digital text” refers to human-authored digital text that cannot be found on a search engine results page, e.g. text inside password-protected social media platforms and forums, direct messaging, email, documents, and enterprise software systems. Fourth, we refer to the sum of these first two corpora as “human-generated digital text,” given that humans authored all of it until recently.
We do not attempt to remove AI-generated text from the second or third corpus, because our objective is merely to compare the total size of these accumulated stocks of text against the pace of output token production. Any AI-generated text already on the indexed public web or inside of private digital text inflates the human side of the ledger, such that our crossover dates are, if anything, conservative. Furthermore, stripping out LLM-generated text from these sources would require AI-detection at enormous scale (and with unknown error rates). We note that our estimates for the size of these two human-dominated textual corpora extrapolate from 2024 estimates based largely on population growth and internet connectivity, so we believe contamination risk to be minimal.
In our conclusion, we find broad evidence that the viral claim from the X post is directionally correct and, if anything, conservative. LLM output tokens generated in 2026 alone likely surpassed total text tokens on the indexed public web by early February of 2026. Output token production accelerated throughout the year, and our estimates imply that LLMs generated an indexed public web’s worth of output tokens every 15 days in 2026. If growth continues at a similar pace, this interval will fall to 1-2 days by 2028.
