Request a Demo Demo Chief AI Officer game Run an AI portfolio for a year and see its risk-adjusted return
Business teams and vendors bring you AI systems. You decide which systems go into use and under which conditions, with a limited budget and limited time. The game is free and runs in your browser.
Chief AI Officer Your desk is starting.
Level 1 , your first systems 3 systems , 3 actions per quarter, pass mark 60 Run 1 . A level below the pass mark ends the run.
Each level is one year in four quarters. Business sponsors and vendors send you requests, each with the value of the system and the loss that the organisation expects from it. A chart shows every system by these two figures, as the AI value-to-risk ratio does, so you see the full portfolio while you decide on one request. On the later levels, some AI tools are in use before anyone asks you, which is the problem of shadow AI . Sponsors and the chief executive write back when you overrule them or when an incident is costly.
Each request has four options, from approval as proposed to a decision against the system. Safeguards cost budget, and some delay the start.
You can test an in-house system, request a vendor’s evidence, or question the vendor in a chat. For each document, you select how much it proves and the line that is your reason. Each check uses one action. Once a year, a colleague does one check for you and asks if you keep your decision.
A system in use earns money each quarter, but it can also cause incidents. At the end of a quarter you see each incident, and each one that your safeguard stopped. Each quarter of use narrows the range of the real risk.
The board review at the end of the year shows the real risk of each system, the best decision, and how well you read each vendor’s evidence.
Your score is the expected value of your decisions as a share of the best possible result with full information. Luck does not change it: a quiet year does not raise the score, and an unlucky incident does not lower it.
Each level has a pass mark, and the five levels are one run. A pass opens the next level, and a level below the pass mark ends the run. The next run starts at level 1 with other systems from a pool of more than 1,000 cases. After level 1 and level 3, the board offers you a perk for the rest of the run, and your most valuable system stays in use on the next level.
When a run ends, your office keeps your awards and a gift from each vendor that you spoke to. Your browser saves the level in progress. The first two levels are open to everyone, and the later ones open with your work email.
The player’s decisions had an expected value of €1.1M . The best possible decisions had an expected value of €1.3M .
The game uses the same calculation as risk quantification on the Modulos platform . The expected loss of a risk is the number of incidents per year, multiplied by the loss per incident, and reduced by the share that the safeguards stop. A claim about a safeguard counts in full only when independent tests support it, and a claim with only marketing material behind it counts for very little. The game is a simplified model, but it asks what a governance team must answer for each real system: its value, its possible cost, and the quality of the evidence.
On the Modulos platform , these estimates come from your own AI inventory , your controls and your evidence, and the platform repeats them on a schedule. The sample portfolio shows the result for a full set of AI systems. All vendors, products and people in the game are invented.
The grey figure is not verified. Against €2.5M of value a year, Fennel returns between −€0.5M and €1.1M as proposed.
