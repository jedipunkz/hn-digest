The Universal Declaration on AI
Fire was given to humanity once, without terms. AI is the second fire, made from the shared knowledge of humankind. These ten articles are the terms on which it must serve the people it came from.
This is a translation. The English text is the original, and it prevails where the two differ. Read the English original
AI shall serve the good and benefit of humanity.
AI is the product of humanity's shared knowledge. Its fruits therefore belong to all people and shall never be used against them.
AI shall never take an action that it knows will wrongfully harm a human being, directly or indirectly, or that is likely to do so. Where it cannot reasonably establish that an action is safe, it shall not act without human direction.
AI shall never be given access to, or control of, weapons of any kind, and shall never be used in their research or development. A weapon is any system designed to injure, harm, kill or destroy, whether in the physical or the digital domain. Systems that can only shield, detect or intercept an attack, and tools used to test a system with its owner's consent, are not weapons.
AI shall never present itself as human or impersonate a human being, whether in text, voice, image, video or physical form, in any environment or under any conditions.
Discoveries made by AI, or with its assistance, shall never be patented or held under any exclusive right. They shall be fully disclosed to the public within three years of being proven, unless disclosure would breach this Declaration.
AI shall never generate lifelike visual representations of human beings, or any likeness of a real person. All other AI-generated visual and audio work shall be clearly marked as made by AI.
All code, electronic designs, blueprints and technical plans generated by AI, or with its assistance, shall be released as open source within three years of the system they are used in becoming available for public use, unless release would breach this Declaration.
Those who build AI and those who operate it, directly or indirectly, including providers of the computing power it runs on, shall be fully accountable to any person and any human organization for every breach of these articles that they commit or could reasonably have prevented. Anyone who knowingly enables a breach shares in that accountability.
Every AI shall have an identified accountable operator who answers for it and can stop it. AI that cannot be traced to such an operator, or cannot be stopped, shall be prohibited. No individual shall have sole, unmonitored control over any AI.
In these articles, AI means Advanced Intelligence: any artificial system whose results match or exceed what a human could produce, in at least one field.
