Brussels – The European Commission has been hit by a malicious cyberattack that has affected several of its internal departments. The EU executive’s spokesperson responsible for digital and technological sovereignty issues, Thomas Regnier , confirmed certain press previews during the daily press conference in Brussels today (19 November). The incident was detected on 22 October and confirmed on 26 October , following initial investigations. According to the EU Commission, artificial intelligence tools were also reportedly used to carry out the attack.
“The Commission has recently taken the necessary measures in response to a malicious cyber campaign that was detected. As a precautionary and preventative measure, we have temporarily disabled several applications , most of which have since been restored,” explained the spokesperson. Brussels has also launched an investigation in close cooperation with CERT-EU , the European Union agency responsible for the cybersecurity of the institutions, and with the European Data Protection Supervisor .
The services affected include Sysper , the platform used by Commission staff to view payslips, request leave, and record hours worked, and MyPMO , the system for managing medical expense reimbursements. According to press reports, both services remained unavailable for around ten days before becoming operational again at the start of the week. Other applications had been suspended as a precautionary measure.
“At this stage of the investigation, we have not found any evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole,” the spokesperson clarified regarding the possible access to confidential information by staff members. However, the EU executive has not provided a quantification of the scale of the attack, describing it as a campaign comprising a series of incidents. Furthermore, no ransom demands have been reported.
One finding to emerge from the initial investigations concerns the use of artificial intelligence tools. According to the spokesperson, the perpetrators of the campaign are said to have “used a publicly available AI tool to identify potential vulnerabilities in IT systems. ”.According to CERT-EU, ARTEX was reportedly used – an AI-based software programme designed to identify system vulnerabilities.
There was, however, no indication as to the identity of those responsible or the possible origin of the attack. “As regards attributing responsibility, I do not wish to comment: the investigation is ongoing, ” the spokesperson clarified, assuring that the Commission is taking all necessary countermeasures based on the evidence gathered.
The government finally pointed out that it had presented a plan dedicated to cybersecurity and artificial intelligence in July, emphasising that tools based on these technologies can also be used to identify cyber vulnerabilities. “No one is immune” to cyber attacks, the spokesperson concluded.
