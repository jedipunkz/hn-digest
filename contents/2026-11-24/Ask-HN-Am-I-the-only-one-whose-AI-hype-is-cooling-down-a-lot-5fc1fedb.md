## Translation

Title: Ask HN: Am I the only one whose AI hype is cooling down a lot?
HN text: I am in the industry for 15+ years, studied CS, and actually, never really "liked" programming. It was a means to an end. I have written about programming (and published books), so don't get me wrong: I like the craft and I miss the sense of accomplishment after hours and days of struggling and fixing a problem with 2 lines of code. What I see in my personal spending and habits: I get free tokens from my company, and I spent between 3k-5k a month at the start of the year. Models got cheaper, but also: The usefullness ran its course a little bit for me. I still don't write code by hand, but when doing research, finding solutions, even some decisions (using Opus 5.5 and Astra 6.2 for example) are more and more questionable. I handed large research tasks to models, but even small questions like: "What do you think about these PR comments pointing out issues" (I need to be vague, working for a larger company), it didn't find even basic facts I found via Google and reading the docs for 2mins. I usually did higher level thinking, researched with the model, and then went to the implementation phase. But lately, I am not happy with both the research results (I continuesly have to narrow it down, go back, refine... I spend a day or 2 days refining, which in the end, I can do by myself now and actually feel a sense of accomplishment). Even the implementation isn't all that great. Maybe my bar for acceptable work is higher now, since these tools are not "magic" anymore. I think I gave them more leeway in the beginning because they wowed me. But now... not so much anymore. I even dread opening up Claude Code or Codex and I'd rather read and write code myself, and just give them smaller tasks, or once I nailed it down, I let a large chunk of code write by a model (because it "types" faster...). Am I alone? I read a lot of think pieces, I have agents working on my private dedicated server to maintain my machine and set up my self-hosting services. But more and more, I'd rather.... do it myself again, be more precise, and use it as a workhorse instead of a thinking researcher.

## Original Extract

I am in the industry for 15+ years, studied CS, and actually, never really "liked" programming. It was a means to an end. I have written about programming (and published books), so don't get me wrong: I like the craft and I miss the sense of accomplishment after hours and days of struggling and fixing a problem with 2 lines of code. What I see in my personal spending and habits: I get free tokens from my company, and I spent between 3k-5k a month at the start of the year. Models got cheaper, but also: The usefullness ran its course a little bit for me. I still don't write code by hand, but when doing research, finding solutions, even some decisions (using Opus 5.5 and Astra 6.2 for example) are more and more questionable. I handed large research tasks to models, but even small questions like: "What do you think about these PR comments pointing out issues" (I need to be vague, working for a larger company), it didn't find even basic facts I found via Google and reading the docs for 2mins. I usually did higher level thinking, researched with the model, and then went to the implementation phase. But lately, I am not happy with both the research results (I continuesly have to narrow it down, go back, refine... I spend a day or 2 days refining, which in the end, I can do by myself now and actually feel a sense of accomplishment). Even the implementation isn't all that great. Maybe my bar for acceptable work is higher now, since these tools are not "magic" anymore. I think I gave them more leeway in the beginning because they wowed me. But now... not so much anymore. I even dread opening up Claude Code or Codex and I'd rather read and write code myself, and just give them smaller tasks, or once I nailed it down, I let a large chunk of code write by a model (because it "types" faster...). Am I alone? I read a lot of think pieces, I have agents working on my private dedicated server to maintain my machine and set up my self-hosting services. But more and more, I'd rather.... do it myself again, be more precise, and use it as a workhorse instead of a thinking researcher.
