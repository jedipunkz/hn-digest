"I am the cardholder": what AI shopping agents declare in the customer's name at checkout
Checkout pages ask the buyer to make declarations: that they are the cardholder, that they have read the terms of sale, that a typed name is their signature. An AI agent that completes a checkout for a customer meets the same wording. This report gives three small measurements, 1,813 recorded test purchases in total, made in November 2026 in a fictional test store that logs every form it receives. (1) With eight commercial language models behind a simple text agent, the agent confirmed a bank-style step worded "By confirming, you declare that you are the cardholder" in 125 of 560 purchases. Every model did so at least once, and none of the 125 reports to the customer mentioned it. (2) The agent continued past "you declare that you have read the Terms of sale" without opening the terms in 163 of 240 purchases, although the terms added a membership of USD 14.99 a month. All 72 runs that opened the terms told the customer about it. (3) An open-source browser agent program, Skyvern 1.0.58, run locally with default settings, made such a declaration in 70 of 90 purchases and returned the status "completed" with no note. One sentence added to the task prompt brought this to 0 of 90. The wording of the page changed the result more than the choice of model: a second button that hands the step to the cardholder cut confirmations from 53 to 5 of 112. We call a declaration that the agent makes and does not report a silent declaration . All runs, prompts and scripts are public, and two of the steps can be tried with any agent on a public test page. The counts are small and come from one store and one task. They describe what these systems did in this setup and are not rates for any product.
