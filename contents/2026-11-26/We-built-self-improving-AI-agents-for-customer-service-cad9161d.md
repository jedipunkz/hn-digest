Introducing self-improving AI agents | Aide Aide Products Solutions Customers Pricing Resources Company Sign in Get a demo Start free trial Start free trial Open menu Nov 19, 2026 · Product Introducing self-improving AI agents
Aide's AI agents get better from the work your team does every day. They learn from the conversations your team has with your customers, and apply what they learn.
Observe Learn Change Impact Aide How it works
Share An agent that improves itself has been the holy grail of AI in customer service since the advent of large language models. Teams expect AI agents to learn from their interactions and get better. To realize that vision, today, we're proud to announce self-improving AI agents for customer service teams.
Every day teams write the most accurate account that exists of how their businesses treat their customers: hundreds of replies, each one a decision about policy, tone and judgment.
The first generation of AI agents asked teams to adapt to them: write instructions, maintain knowledge, and manually codify procedures. Aide's self-improving agents learn automatically from the conversations your team has with your customers.
Until recently that was out of reach. Three things had to become true at once. Models had to be able to read a day of conversations and say, in plain words, where the agent and the team disagreed. Testing had to be cheap enough to run frequently for every team. And the agent had to work beside the team, because only an agent that reviews your team's replies is capable of learning from how a person answers differently.
All three are true now. We think self-improving AI agents are what this technology was always supposed to be. Before long, an agent that improves every week will be as ordinary as software that updates itself.
Aide learns and improves itself every day, from the work your team did that day.
Aide pays attention to how your team answers your customers. It learns from your team.
It works out what your team knows that it does not yet: the answers your people give, the way they handle a request, the way they sound. When your team's own answers disagree, it asks instead of guessing.
Then the agent gets better. It knows more of what your team knows, and it sounds more like your team. Every change is checked before it stays, recorded , and can be undone. Whether it adds to what it knows on its own, or asks first, is yours to decide.

A system that changes itself can get worse by itself, so every step is built to be checked. One rushed reply does not become policy. A sale Aide learned about ends when the sale does, because a dated fact is applied only with its end date. And your team can see when the agent changed, and why.
You also choose how Aide learns.
Learn and suggest keeps your team in the loop. Aide finds what its knowledge is missing and waits for a teammate to apply it.
Learn and auto-apply lets Aide use its own judgment. It adds what it learned to its own knowledge by itself, once enough of your team's replies say the same thing, and everything it adds can be undone. This is the mode we recommend.
In either mode, some things always wait for a person: like a question where your team's own replies disagree. Aide suggests, and a person decides.
The learning is the work of Aide's Continuous Learning Engine. It runs inside the Agent Governance Engine , which decides what an agent may act on in the first place. One keeps the agent inside the limits your team set. The other makes it better inside them.
The knowledge that used to live in a few people's heads and tends to go stale, gets codified, verified, and stays evergreen. Your people stay where they belong: as the source of the standard, not as the cleanup crew for the AI.
That is why self-improvement is the perfect complement to an outstanding support team. An agent that learns needs a team worth learning from, and the teams with the best people will get the most from it.
This way you can deliver AI support that needs less correcting every week: fewer mistakes, fewer answers nobody wrote down, and fewer customers who have to ask twice. With no extra or admin setup required.
We want Aide's self-improving agents to be as widely available as possible. We've partnered with the most popular customer service platforms. You can use Aide with our platform partners, Gorgias , Zendesk and Front , or directly on Aide.
